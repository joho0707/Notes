## Notes

- Electrical computing began in the late-1930s
- History of computing is viewed as stages (including generations)
- 1st-Gen relied on vacuum tubes/valves - glass tubes with circuits inside [1946 - 1956]
  - Memory size roughly 2000 bytes (2kb) 10,000 instructions per second
  - Rotating metal drums used for internal storage - punch cards for external storage
- 2nd-Gen - transistors replace valves - semiconductor amplifiers [1957 - 1963]
  - smaller, better, faster, stronger than vacuum tubes
  - 200,000 - 300,000 instructions per second
- 3rd-Gen - integrated circuits - thousands of layers of transistors on silicon chips (semiconductors) [1964 - 1979]
  - 2MB of Random Access Memory (RAM)
  - 5 million instructions per second
  - Operating systems needed - made it easier for people with less experience to use
  - Intel 4004 - microchip
- 4th-Gen - large-scale integrated circuits [1980 - present]
  - Costs fall - desktop computers relatively inexpensive
  - 8GB memory is standard
